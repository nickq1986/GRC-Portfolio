# Nicholas Quinn | GRC Portfolio 1 Academic Analysis and Design

This portfolio brings together academic projects in governance, risk, compliance, and cybersecurity. Across numerous case studies, I explore how to identify and assess risk, select and justify controls, assign accountability, and make security decisions understandable to an organization.

I built these projects to turn course concepts into practical examples and to develop a connected view of GRC work: from understanding an organization’s context, through risk analysis and control selection, to treatment, oversight, and future improvement.

## Projects

### 1. ISO 27001 for an Energy Company — Part A

An information governance and cybersecurity assessment covering organizational scope, governance responsibilities, ISO/IEC 27001, risk identification, qualitative analysis, evaluation, and treatment options.

**Why I built it:** To practice translating an organization’s information governance needs into a structured, risk-based assessment aligned with an established security management standard.

[Read the Part A project](./ISO%2027001%20For%20Energy%20Company%20Part%20A.md)

### 2. ISO 27001 for an Energy Company — Part B

A collaborative continuation with academic peers focused on implementing the response: responsibilities, control justification, a risk treatment plan, and monitoring mechanisms.

**Why I built it:** Risk analysis is useful only when it supports decisions and follow-through. This project develops the link between identified risks, selected controls, accountable owners, and ongoing oversight.

[Read the Part B project](./ISO%2027001%20For%20Energy%20Company%20Part%20B%20-%20Copy.md)

### 3. NIST CSF and CIS Controls for a Software Development Company

A security review and improvement proposal for a software development scenario. It brings together security risks, threat modelling, OSINT, MITRE ATT&CK analysis, defence in depth, governance, and Active Directory identity and access management.

**Why I built it:** To explore how a software company can connect its threat landscape and technical environment to a coherent governance and control strategy.

[Read the NIST CSF and CIS Controls project](./NIST%20CSF%20and%20CIS%20controls%20for%20a%20Software%20Development%20Company.md)

### 4. Networking Controls for a Healthcare Company

A healthcare network design and control review covering segmentation, VLANs, inter-VLAN routing, DMZs, wireless and port security, domain services, WAN connectivity, VPNs, and network security principles.

**Why I built it:** To examine how network architecture choices support confidentiality, integrity, availability, least privilege, and defence in depth in a context where access to systems and information matters.

[Read the networking controls project](./Networking%20Controls%20For%20a%20Healthcare%20Company.md)

### 5. Cloud Security Controls for a Travel Company

A cloud and big-data case study covering migration and deployment choices, risk assessment, identity and access management, S3 storage, EC2 compute, encryption, security groups, access analysis, and security dashboards.

**Why I built it:** To consider how cloud services can support business and analytics goals while keeping security, risk ownership, and social, ethical, and legal responsibilities in view.

[Read the cloud security project](./Cloud%20Security%20Controls%20for%20a%20Travel%20Company.md)

### 6. AI Governance for Predictive Risk Intelligence

A dissertation examining organizational readiness to integrate predictive risk intelligence, including research on governance, accuracy and bias, privacy, transparency, accountability, security, leadership, and implementation barriers.

**Why I built it:** Predictive tools can affect important risk decisions. This research explores the organizational capabilities and governance needed to evaluate their use responsibly.

[Read the AI governance dissertation](./Dissertation%20-%20AI%20Govenance%20for%20Predictive%20Risk%20Intelligence.md)

## How the projects fit together

The energy-company work moves from risk assessment in Part A to treatment and monitoring in Part B. The software, healthcare, and travel-company projects apply related GRC thinking to different technical environments. The dissertation extends the portfolio into emerging technology governance and organizational readiness.

Together, the projects show how I approach GRC as an ongoing process: understand the context, identify and assess risk, choose and explain controls, assign responsibility, and review whether the response remains appropriate.

## What I would improve next

The projects so far focus on analysis and design. The next step is turning decisions into policy that people can follow, which is the focus of GRC Portfolio 2: Policy Writing. I also plan to update the framework work to NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022, and to add a worked example of an internal audit.

## Scope and responsible use

These documents are academic and portfolio work based on case-study scenarios. They are not independent audits, certifications, production security assessments, or implementation guarantees. Recommendations should be adapted to an organization’s actual environment, risk appetite, and applicable requirements before use.

# Nicholas Quinn | GRC Portfolio 2: Policy Writing

Portfolio 1 focuses on risk assessment, control selection and governance analysis. This second portfolio focuses on the step that turns those decisions into something an organization can follow: writing clear, usable policy.

It will be a set of sample information security and governance policies written for fictional organizations. Each policy is written to be read by the people who have to follow it, with the control objectives, responsibilities and enforcement stated plainly.

**Status:** In progress. Completed policies are marked below and linked once published.

## Why I'm building it

My technical background is in security operations and vulnerability management, and my academic work covers frameworks, risk and governance. What I have not yet done in a workplace is write policy. This portfolio is how I am building that skill in the open: choosing a standard, deciding what a policy should and should not say, and showing the reasoning.
