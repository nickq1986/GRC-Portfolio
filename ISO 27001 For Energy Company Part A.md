Executive Summary

247E&G is a family-owned limited company, with its HQ located in London. It operates in the UK and therefor the scale of the business is comprised of its customer base within major domestic cities and its stakeholders – predominantly regional and national transmission networks. Connectivity, is a key aspect of the current business model, harmonising its operations with its stakeholders. However, recent environmental challenges have highlighted inefficiencies, regarding connectivity at the business output level, affecting its customers base posing concerns over the risk of revenue loss. The main challenge to the current business model is rising cost of energy prices. The effects of geopolitical and macroeconomic events on energy costs, and the impact of these costs on the price of the company’s deliverables, propose a clear risk of business loss in a highly competitive domestic energy market. Another challenge stems from a customer relations management (CRM) perspective. Current economic instability, resulting in public anxiety concerning the ‘energy crises’ has created an ever-greater demand for even family owned limited companied to maintain channels of communication and interfaces with its customer base, to deal with a daily, overwhelming influx of customer queries. 247E&G aims to introduce measures, intended to manage the cost of its deliverables, and enhance its services. This strategy will be achieved following the successful completion of the following objectives:

1. Provision of a system that customises suitable energy packages based on energy usage. This will be an automated process, whereby the customer can submit a meter reading and manage online payments. This achieves the business aim by empowering the customer base to manage their energy costs on a need basis.

2. Implementation of a web-based interface, this will be a technology-based innovation of the current CRM strategy, that allows customers a single point of access, and act as a self-service option. This achieves the business aim by simplifying operations and improving the customer experience.

This is a data driven strategy to enhance the current business model. 247E&G acknowledges its regulatory, legal, and contractual obligations for the business, to safeguard the privacy of their customer’s data. The organisations dependency on critical national infrastructure means that breaches in IS pose the risk of disrupting 247E&Gs business operations, but as the business implements its strategy and assumes the ownership of much of this risk, it recognises it now has a much greater level of responsibility to protect the personal data of its customers and stakeholders in the current threat environment outlined by the NCSC. The organisation understands that this will be achieved by building an effective information governance framework.

## **Introduction**

WannaCry has become synonymous with cyber threats to UK Critical National Infrastructure (CNI). The investigating report indicated that none of the NHS organisations affected by the attack had the Microsoft patch update advised by NHS Digital following threat intelligence reports less than a month prior to the attack

The findings and the opinions expressed from external investigation are indicative of the pressures faced by organisations. Whilst the threat environment is a variable outside of an organisations control, it is clear to see that the organisation itself is held accountable for short-comings in its Information Governance (IG) policy. The true gravity of poor IG implementation was realised by British Airways, when infringement of General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) accrued the company record breaking fines

Despite the threat environment, the risks, and the penalties for non-compliance, statistics show that many organisations do not implement IG policies against information security management system standards . From an Information Security (IS) perspective, this is a common pitfall that 247E&G should avoid.

## **4.Frameworks**

### 4.1 Information Governance Principles

Organisational Governance concepts – Govern, manage Risk, Compliance (GRC) are transferred into the area of Information Management (IM), and implemented into an IG GRC concept framework . 247E&G’s organisational objectives are driven by a competitive electricity market and the need to operate in a supply chain. Therefore, the IG policy must enable these objectives. However. IM stakeholders have subjective areas of responsibility, each carrying their respective risks to the IG objectives (Figure 4). Therefore, the overarching framework practices must manage these risks in compliance with 247E&G’s contractual, regulatory, and legal obligations holistically, to enable the IG policy objectives.

### 4.2 Risk Management and Compliance

247E&G’s objectives coincide with the broader UK net zero strategy. The UK’s national response to the risks has been focused on a critical review of cyber resilience frameworks. The government proposes that CNI organisations adopt the Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF)

![Embedded image](<assets/ISO 27001 For Energy Company - image 01.png>)

CAF was developed by the NCSC, to assist government organisations, regulators and industry sectors comply with the NIS-r, by providing a systematic method of assessing the extent to which an organization is adequately managing cybersecurity risks in relation to the delivery of essential services

CAF is designed as a framework to be mapped against NIS-r principles and objectives (Figure 5) and used for self-assessment or external review by independent bodies such as Competent Authorities (CA) . This is a relevant to 247E&G, as it meets the criteria of an Operator of Essential Services (OES) under schedule 2 of the NIS-r, therefore it must demonstrate compliance with section 10, to its respective CA -Ofgem. However, the scalability of strategic automated web-based process, and the potential for third party involvement in its tariff customisation function, is likely to bring 247E&G into the scope of section 12(1)c of the NIS-r, which encourages the use of international standards for Relevant Digital Service Providers (RDSP) to manage the risks to the availability of their essential services.

Regardless of this development, 247E&G is a data controller, and to comply with the DPA 2018, they must adopt a holistic approach to information risk management, which records of all categories of its processing activities, and protects the CIA of all sensitive information. Therefore, 247E&G should follow the NIS-r guidance and adopt an International Standard which is relevant to them and concerned with information holistically such as the ISO/IEC 27001.

This ISMS framework includes controls for the subjective areas of stakeholder responsibility (Figure 4) and has an approach to cyber resilience which is consistent with the NIS-r , making it a preferably justifiable prospective framework for the companies IG policy.

### 4.3 ISO/IEC 27001

The International Organisation of Standardization was developed specifically for international-wide assurance by technical committees comprising of experts , and the ISO/IEC 27001 (Hereafter the standard) was commonly adopted for the design, implementation, and monitoring of an ISMS.

The standard follows the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PCDA) cycle, which is a well-known model for continuous improvement through corrective actions . This provides a framework for the planning, implementation and monitoring mechanisms, to address the security threats and mitigate the security vulnerabilities faced by 247E&G.  summarises this as:

**Plan** what you need to do to achieve the objective.

**Do **what you Planned.

**Check **that what you have done achieves what you had planned for it to achieve and identify any gaps or shortfalls.

**Act** on the findings of the check phase to address the gaps and/or improve the efficiency and effectiveness of what you have in place.

Retention of documented information demonstrates compliance with the standard.   identifies the relevant documentation. This includes the scope which must define the boundaries of 247E&G’s ISMS along with the policy and objectives which must be reviewed to ensure that the control measures implemented are appropriate to the organisational context. Information asset inventories and registers should be maintained and constantly reviewed and updated, and asset owners should be included in the appointed security roles and responsibilities as part of the risk management process. These requirements also specify a detailed report which describes the risk management plan with separate documents for risk assessments and risk treatments plans and methodologies used to determine suitable controls for the ISMS, and a statement of applicability to provide a roadmap for the controls implemented.

This gives 247E&G a market advantage because CNI organisations take contractual steps to mandate security standards for electricity suppliers  and this documentation provides a means of systematic external review for monitoring and certification of compliance to internationally recognised information security best practices.

## **5. Risk Assessment Methodology**

Risk acceptance criteria was established with considerations for the company’s business model and strategy. This included the introduction of automated processes in the previously examined threat climate, with the requirements to maintain network security, the CIA of sensitive information and the availability of essential services to comply with contractual, regulatory, and legal obligations. This section outlines the criteria for performing Risk Assessments in compliance with clause 6.1.2 of the standard, including the methodologies to ensure that repeated information security risk assessments produce consistent, valid, and comparable results.

### 5.1 Risk Identification

Company assets were categorised by information asset types according to . This included analysis of current system components, as well as modelling of future systems that included automated CRM and payment processes and the identification of current asset owners and likely owners of the strategies projected assets.

The report has speculated that 247E&G will face threats to its systems, networks, and sensitive customer centric information assets. Therefore, threat modelling was carried out along the dimensions of network security (STRIDE) using the OWASP threat modelling tool. Additional threats such outside of STRIDE such as human errors, environmental hazards such as adverse weather conditions and application errors were also included in the threat modelling process.

Vulnerabilities highlighted in section 3 as a caveat to technological innovations compounded by research into the social environment and 247E&G’s independent review of the company’s current training and awareness competencies indicate the following vulnerabilities.

Technical cyber resilience

Information handling procedures

Business continuity in the event of major incidents

Access control in the social environment
